### What the matrix shows

- **Chat over your finances is now table stakes in the US.** Cleo, Monarch, Rocket Money (Rowan), Origin and ChatGPT itself all ship it. YNAB answered through ChatGPT instead of building it in-app [19]. Every US example runs on bank-synced data (Plaid or similar). None of them answer from an on-device aggregated snapshot, and that approach is a privacy point Moni can claim.
- **Natural-language, voice and receipt entry is where the new AI-native trackers compete** (Rolly, DrakeAI, ChatExpense, and Cleo's voice). These features are cheap to copy, so they do not protect Moni on their own.
- **SMS or notification parsing is concentrated in India** (Axio, Moneyview, FinArt, PennyWise [46]) and in a few small Malaysian apps (Duitful [41]; xFin and SnappySpend claim notification auto-capture, unverified [47]). No large Western app does it, because those markets rely on bank sync.
- **No app found combines** notification capture with history-matched auto-add, duplicate and transfer-leg detection, and an AI chat. Duitful is the closest Malaysian competitor, but it requires approval for every entry and has no chat.

## 3. Southeast Asia and Malaysia

### Open banking status (BNM)

- **Not live today.** BNM issued an Exposure Draft on Open Finance on 18 Nov 2025. Feedback closed on 1 Mar 2026 [48].
- **Scope:** mandated sharing of "account and transaction information of deposit account, e-money account, charge card, and credit card" [48].
- **Phased timeline (draft):** banks with more than 1M individual customers by Jan 2027. Banks with more than 100k individual or SME customers by Jan 2028. Development financial institutions and e-money issuers with more than 5M active users by Jan 2029 [48]. The e-money-issuer phase is the one that would cover Touch 'n Go, GrabPay and similar wallets.
- **BNM Governor, July 2026:** "Our Open Finance framework establishes a foundation for secure, consent-based data sharing … with supporting infrastructure being developed with PayNet and the industry, and phased implementation from 2027" [49].
- **Unverified:** whether non-licensed third parties such as a PFM app can become data consumers, and on what terms. The law-firm summaries I read do not say [48][50]. A final policy document had not been found as of this research. One commentary expects it in Q3–Q4 2026 (unverified) [51].
- **Current workarounds are weak.** One vendor says "third-party aggregator coverage of Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank and RHB is inconsistent and can break without notice" [38]. Buxfer lists a Maybank (MY) sync connector [52].
- **E-wallet blind spot:** "a RM100 reload shows in your bank as one RM100 transfer" [38]. Even after open finance arrives, wallet-level purchases may not be visible until the 2029 e-money issuer phase.

### What Malaysians use

- **Seedly** (Singapore, under MoneyHero Group) discontinued its app on 31 Dec 2025. Expense data is deleted after 31 Mar 2026 [53]. Its exit leaves a gap in the regional market.
- **Built-in bank and wallet trackers:** MAE by Maybank has an "automated expense tracker" covering Maybank activity only [54][55]. The TNG eWallet Play listing advertises spending tracking inside TNG [56].
- **Global apps used locally:** Money Lover (a Malaysian review exists [57]), Spendee, Wallet, Wally, and YNAB. BNM's older "My Tabung" app is also listed (2018 article, maintenance unverified) [44][58].
- **Local AI or MY-specific apps:** Duitful, RichAuntie, Finory, RinggitWise and Rolly (above).
- **Market share:** no figures found. Unverified.

### Does anyone parse Malaysian bank or e-wallet notifications?

**Yes, but only small players.**

- **Duitful** (Android, open beta per search snippet) parses notifications from Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank, RHB, Hong Leong, HSBC, Bank Islam, TNG, GrabPay, Boost, BigPay, ShopeePay and BNPL apps. Parsing runs on-device and the user approves each entry. iOS is "coming soon" without this feature, because "iOS doesn't allow notification reading" [41].
- **xFin** (Malaysian developer, updated Oct 2026) and **SnappySpend** (added a "Malaysia app" in June 2026) advertise notification auto-capture. Both are unverified: the Play pages could not be fetched, and the claims come from search snippets only [47].
- **RichAuntie** (iOS) uses screenshots with AI extraction, not notifications [42].
- **No app found does SMS parsing for Malaysian banks.** This fits the market: BNM ordered banks off SMS OTP in Sept 2022 [59][60]. Maybank has moved alert types from SMS to MAE push notifications in stages: failed card transactions in 2022, debit purchases over RM500 for app users in 2020, and limit or profile changes in Apr 2024 [61][62]. In Malaysia, **push notifications, not SMS, are where transaction data now shows up**, and only Android lets an app read them.

**Moat verdict:** notification capture alone is **not** a moat. At least one direct Malaysian competitor ships it, and parsing rules are copyable. A partial moat would come from what Moni builds on top of it: breadth and robustness of parsers, history-matched auto-add, transfer and duplicate pairing, and multi-currency. Open finance will not cover e-wallets until about 2029 [48], so notification capture keeps an advantage for e-wallet spending for about 2–3 years.

## 4. Platform constraints

### Google Play: SMS permissions

Policy: "Use of SMS or Call Log permission groups" [63].

- READ_SMS and RECEIVE_SMS are for **default SMS, Phone or Assistant handlers**. Other apps may only get an exception approved through the **Permissions Declaration Form**. "Apps must be actively registered as the default SMS, Phone, or Assistant handler before prompting users" applies to the default-handler path [63].
- **Exceptions that apply to finance apps:** "SMS-based money management (for example, apps that track and manage budget)" and "SMS-based financial transactions (e.g. UPI)". Both are "subject to Google Play review and approval" [63].
- **Limit on data use:** "personal loans or budgeting apps may not exfiltrate or share non-financial or personal SMS history of a user" [63].
- **Grounds for rejection or removal:** no Permissions Declaration Form, no qualifying core use, or deceptive or undeclared use. Possible results are removal, suspension, or developer account termination [63].
- One competitor blog reports some SMS trackers have been removed from Play (3p, unverified) [46].
- **For Moni:** SMS adds little in Malaysia (see section 3) and brings review risk. Notifications are the better input channel.

### Google Play: NotificationListenerService

- **No restricted-permission declaration found.** BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE is not covered on the "Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information" policy page [64]. Unverified whether reviewers ask for justification in practice.
- **General policy rules still apply:**
  - Request permissions only when "necessary to implement current features or services" [64].
  - **User Data policy: prominent disclosure.** The disclosure must be in-app and shown during normal use. It must describe the data and how it is used or shared. It "cannot only be placed in a privacy policy." It "must be immediately preceded by" the consent or runtime request [65].
  - "Financial and payment information" is personal and sensitive data [65].
  - **"These requirements also apply to third-party AI integrations"** [65]. This bears on sending parsed notification text to an LLM.
  - The Data safety section must match the app's behavior [66].
- **Play Protect:** sideloaded apps using NOTIFICATION_LISTENER, READ_SMS, RECEIVE_SMS or ACCESSIBILITY are "considered high-risk applications because these permissions are frequently abused for financial fraud" [67]. Allowed examples given are wearables relaying notifications, notification aggregators, and launchers or widgets. Budgeting is not named [67]. This affects EAS internal or APK distribution, not Play-installed builds.
- **OS-level limits:**
  - Android 13 "Restricted settings" blocks enabling notification access for sideloaded apps until the user overrides it. Android 15 adds Enhanced Confirmation Mode [68][69].
  - Android 15 OTP redaction: "Android will stop untrusted apps that implement a NotificationListenerService from reading unredacted content from notifications where an OTP has been detected" [70]. Bank notifications that carry a TAC or OTP will arrive redacted. Ordinary transaction alerts are unaffected, but this is unverified for mixed alert-plus-code messages.

### iOS: why notifications cannot be read

- Apple DTS engineer: "your app cannot access any other notifications, like text messages, in the Notification Center … It is not possible for your app to intercept and read notifications from other apps." The only route is ANCS for BLE accessories, "You cannot do this with an app" [71].
- **SMS:** the only API is the Message Filter extension. It receives "the body of a message received from an unknown sender" for spam filtering only, not for known senders like banks [72].
- **Result:** Duitful [41] and FinArt [46] fall back to manual entry or screenshots on iOS. RichAuntie uses Back Tap screenshots [42].
- **For Moni:** iOS parity needs screenshot or share-sheet capture or statement import.

**For Moni:** the two biggest documented causes of abandonment are manual-entry burden (57% in the only peer-reviewed finance-specific data) and broken bank sync (21% of critical reviews). Notification capture with auto-add targets the first. Avoiding bank APIs sidesteps the second, but it creates a matching risk: parser breakage when banks change notification text. Guilt or discomfort (5.4%) is a caution against leaning too hard on "roast" tone.
