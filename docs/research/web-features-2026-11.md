# Desktop features competitors have that Moni lacks (November 2026)

This page answers one question for the Moni web app: which advanced features do competing finance apps offer on the web or desktop that Moni does not support yet? The owner wants the web app to offer more than the phone, not the phone widened.

Research done on 2026-11-18. Sources are Mobbin web screens (Monarch, Copilot Money, YNAB, Rocket Money, Quicken, Origin, Mercury, Xero, Expensify) and vendor product pages, help centres and changelogs. Every claim links to a page that was opened or a Mobbin screen that was looked at. Competitor facts go out of date; check before you rely on a detail.

## Summary

The biggest gap is time. Moni shows the current month well, but its competitors' web apps are built around how money moves over months and years: net worth over time, a projected balance for the weeks ahead, month-against-month trends, a yearly view, and an income-to-spending flow diagram. Moni already has nearly all the data for these. Balances come from wallets and transactions, bills and payday come from the on-device recurring detector, and categories are clean thanks to review. Most of these gaps need no schema change, only new views. The second gap is planning: savings goals, budgets that start on payday, rollover, and yearly budgets for costs like road tax and Raya. These need small schema additions. The third gap is a capture route only a desktop suits: importing a bank or card PDF statement to fill in what notifications missed. Recommendation: build the time views first (net worth, forecast, money calendar, trends, cash flow), because they are cheap, read-only, and use a big screen best. Then add goals and the budget-period changes. The brief's "no Reports page" rule means the charts must live on Home, Activity and Budget, not on a new tab.

## What Moni already has (not repeated below)

Wallets with multi-currency, transactions with notes and a receipt image (`receiptImageUrl` in `packages/types/src/transaction.ts`), categories, monthly category budgets, bills from recurring detection with reminders (`recurring_overrides`), payday detection for chat (`apps/mobile/lib/ai/facts/index.ts`), person-to-person debts, AI capture with Review, auto-add rules, merchant names, the places map, Moni chat, CSV import and export, and balance correction. The round 2 web brief already covers: table view, saved views, bulk edit, inline edit, Ctrl K, keyboard review, rule preview, budget table with category history, people table, chat acting on many rows, customisable Home, CSV import mapping, and a merchant table.

## The top 8 High-fit gaps in Moni's web app

### 1. Net worth over time

The phone already has this: the Wallets page's net worth chart (1M to All, Total | By wallet, `components/wallets/net-worth-chart.tsx`, `lib/finance/balance-history.ts`). The web gap is the richer desktop form, not the chart itself. Keep the chart's one home on Wallets (owner's rule: one home per chart), and add there what the phone has no room for: grouped assets and liabilities, debts as their own lines, per-wallet change, and Download CSV. Compute each wallet's balance on each day from `initialBalanceMinor` plus its transactions, balance corrections included. Group the totals: cash, banks and e-wallets, `investment`, minus `credit`. Owed-to-me and I-owe debts can show as their own lines. Show one line per currency, or a converted total once gap 13 exists. No schema change. Look at the [Monarch accounts page](https://mobbin.com/screens/aaf74c3f-e1df-4376-b05b-cb4a9b290ee2) (chart above grouped accounts, assets and liabilities summary, "Download CSV") and [Copilot](https://mobbin.com/screens/2d7f5bba-90ee-4a0f-a675-7683b5aa512f) (account detail pane with its own chart, 1W to ALL ranges).

### 2. Projected balance to payday and beyond

Add it to Home beside net worth, and to Budget as a "Left after bills" line. Start from today's balance and add the expected bills from `selectUpcomingBills` and the detected payday from chat facts. Let the user untick a bill or income to see the effect, as in Xero's side panel. Mark the forecast as dashed from today, so it never reads as real data. No schema change; the logic in `apps/mobile/lib/finance/bills.ts` and `lib/ai/facts` must move to a shared package so the web can run it. Look at [Xero](https://mobbin.com/screens/d7179a06-0195-4f94-97a9-19e781e3f50e) (money in, money out, projected end balance, editable bill list) and [Quicken](https://mobbin.com/screens/58828f93-c537-4b8f-a409-67192717f1b5) (account picker beside the projection).

### 3. Money calendar

Show it as a view on Activity, with Bills as a filter. Each day cell shows spending, income and bills due; clicking a day filters the table to that day. Data is already there (transactions, bills, `skipPeriod`, `dueOnce`). No schema change. Look at [Monarch Recurring](https://mobbin.com/screens/5519377d-2823-4bc1-917a-f5cb9dd74ebb) (list and calendar toggle, paid and remaining above the grid) and [Origin Home](https://mobbin.com/screens/b0ef86f3-b1c6-473b-ac88-982279d52f2b) (daily spend grid beside recent transactions).

### 4. Cash flow: where the money went

Show it as a view on Activity, beside the running-totals chart from the brief, not as a Reports tab. On the left are income sources (salary, side income by merchant), in the middle category groups, and on the right categories. Below it, a month table shows income, spent and net. Transfers, debt cash movements and corrections stay out, as in all analysis today. No schema change. Look at [Monarch](https://mobbin.com/screens/92c2b32c-20a0-4487-9b6c-3f32cb464893) (three-level Sankey, savings rate tile, saved views) and [Origin](https://mobbin.com/screens/33ec4719-0eac-4763-9b49-65f6aee928b6) (the month table under the diagram).

### 5. Trends and comparisons

Put these on Budget (each category's change against last month and its 3-month average) and on Activity (a "compare to" range). The trend notes are written from computed facts, not by a model: "Food RM 412 this month, RM 96 above your 3-month average". The yearly view is the same view with the range set to a year. No schema change. Look at [Mercury Insights](https://mobbin.com/screens/10e9460a-1e44-4361-afe6-e0e9d857e6d9) (trend sentences beside the chart, compare-to, range scrubber), [Rocket Money](https://mobbin.com/screens/2c672084-8391-462c-b8d1-44fe11646973) (change column per category, frequent spend), and [Monarch](https://mobbin.com/screens/5ccd25f4-5075-497d-93ad-d742ae49345b) (Totals or Change toggle).

### 6. Savings goals

Add a Goals section under Budget (subpages stay under their tab). A goal has a name, target, currency, optional date, and one or more wallets; progress is those wallets' balance or their net inflow since the goal started. Status (on track, behind) compares progress with a straight line to the date. Needs a `goals` table, a Zod schema in `packages/types`, and a migration. Do not copy Monarch's "Allocate funds" or Quicken's "Contribute / Withdraw", which move money between buckets. Look at [Monarch goal detail](https://mobbin.com/screens/97c05fc9-d9d9-46f3-b496-917689081e9f) (saved, spent, left to save, timeline) and [Quicken](https://mobbin.com/screens/d1c178b9-d12e-4740-ab0c-3f477d5a8740) (card grid with "Ahead 1 month").

### 7. Budgets that fit Malaysian money: payday start, rollover, yearly lines

Add these to Budget settings and to the brief's budget table. There are three changes. First, a budget start day taken from the detected payday, stored in `preferences`. Second, a per-category rollover flag on `category_budgets`. Third, a `yearly` period with a due month for road tax, insurance and Raya. Each change needs a schema change. Rollover moves only a category's own leftover into its own next month; there is no "cover overspending from another category". Look at [Copilot settings](https://mobbin.com/screens/761f8bd5-24e6-4755-82ee-65a73f8be7d4) (rollover on, start month, which categories), [Monarch budget](https://mobbin.com/screens/fe7beadd-310d-4122-b68f-6a135a217001) (non-monthly section, "Every 6 months", rollover badge), and [YNAB](https://mobbin.com/screens/b39d62d0-0d5e-4d65-b13c-ce4f48416977) (yearly target editor in a side pane).

### 8. Statement import

Add this to the CSV import screen from the brief. The user drops a bank or card PDF statement; the Go backend extracts its rows into `proposed_transactions`; the existing pairing marks rows that match captured transactions, so only the missing ones go to Review. Needs a new Go endpoint and a new value in `transactionSourceSchema` (the Source slot, such as "Statement"). OpenRouter cost is per statement, not per row. This is the desktop's own answer to "silent capture failure", the top threat in the review research. No Mobbin screen showed this; evidence is [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/11-lunch-money-features-you-should-be-using-and-why) (import from spreadsheet or PDF) and [Spendee](https://www.spendee.com/support) (file import is web only).

## What Moni should deliberately not do

- **Bank login aggregation.** Malaysia has no Plaid, and broken sync is the top complaint wherever it exists (review research). Every forecast, net worth and goal above works from captured data.
- **Zero-based "assign every ringgit", "Cover overspending", "Allocate funds".** [YNAB](https://mobbin.com/screens/60e3dc69-52d3-4bd4-a52d-e241826610f2) and [Monarch goals](https://mobbin.com/screens/d1f47a94-1ee5-48a3-bd7f-a3faa527acb7) work by moving money between categories or goals. The owner rejected moving money between budgets.
- **Splits, tags, and events.** [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/11-lunch-money-features-you-should-be-using-and-why) leans on splitting, grouping and tags, and [Expensify](https://mobbin.com/screens/d13994d8-29be-49f3-aad2-0a01afb98d74) reports by tag. All are rejected for Moni; Source holds the tag slot.
- **Full-access household invites.** [Monarch](https://mobbin.com/screens/8d2fbc64-8041-48e7-ba16-47ce3aba63a2) warns that invitees see everything. Moni's RLS is per user, and its privacy promise is part of adoption. If sharing comes, a single shared wallet (as in [Spendee](https://www.spendee.com/support)) is the shape to study.
- **Retirement, equity and Monte Carlo forecasting.** [Monarch](https://mobbin.com/screens/90c787f4-57ef-4d25-ba9f-dbd2f532046c) and [Actual](https://actualbudget.org/docs/reports/) do this from investment data Moni lacks. Keep the forecast to bills and payday.
- **A dashboard builder.** [Actual](https://actualbudget.org/docs/reports/) lets users build many dashboards. The brief's customisable Home is enough; more is a maintenance cost.
- **An advisor persona with memory.** [Origin](https://mobbin.com/screens/80bb3202-ed85-4ebe-b270-541804cc0350) offers an "AI financial advisor" with a profile, and [Copilot](https://www.copilot.money/dispatch/web-app) lets users pick a personality. The review research shows forced or chatty AI loses users; keep Moni chat optional, grounded and docked.
- **US-only extras.** Credit score, bill negotiation and brokerage (seen in [Rocket Money](https://mobbin.com/screens/a391c310-1020-49e2-ae06-923b32645d4e) and [Origin](https://mobbin.com/screens/b5cd5c3d-a76c-4956-b457-6380ca39c408) navigation) have no Malaysian equivalent.

## Limits of this research

- Monarch's help centre and PocketGuard's site refused automated fetches. Claims about them use only Monarch's blog and Mobbin screens. PocketGuard's debt payoff planner is not listed because its page could not be read.
- No Malaysian competitor found has a web app. Duitful, the closest competitor in the review research, could not be found in web search under that name. Seedly closed in 2025.
- Mobbin screens show sample data and may be older than the live product.
- Effort guesses assume the web app reuses the mobile finance logic through a shared package. That move is itself work and is not counted in the estimates.
